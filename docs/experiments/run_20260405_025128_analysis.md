## Analysis

### Phase 1 (Epochs 1-5): Landmark Warmup — Excellent

Landmark head converged quickly and effectively:
- Landmark loss: 0.320 -> 0.090 (72% reduction)
- Val landmark px: 1.08 -> 0.60 px (44% reduction)
- No overfitting: train/val gap minimal
- Gaze angular error constant at ~42° (expected — gaze head frozen)

**Assessment**: Phase 1 performed as designed. Landmark accuracy well-established before gaze introduction.

### Phase 2 (Epochs 6-15): Gaze + Multi-View — Partial Success

**Gaze learning was rapid but plateaued early:**
- Train angular: 42° -> 13.7° (67% reduction)
- Val angular: 42° -> 17.7° (58% reduction)
- Val angular plateaued at ~18° after epoch 8 (3 epochs into Phase 2)

**Landmark accuracy maintained:**
- Val LM_px recovered from 0.68px (epoch 6) to 0.53px (epoch 15)
- Multi-task learning did not degrade landmark performance

**Multi-view losses were inert:**
- `gaze_mv` remained flat at 0.19-0.20 throughout (never decreased)
- `shape` increased slightly from 0.054 to 0.074
- Neither loss provided meaningful learning signal

**Overfitting emerged:**
- Train/val angular gap: 1.8° (epoch 8) -> 4.0° (epoch 15)
- Val angular stopped improving while train angular continued decreasing

### Phase 3 (Epochs 16-20): Fine-Tuning — No Improvement

- Train angular continued decreasing: 13.6° -> 12.6°
- Val angular stagnated at 18.1-18.3° (actually got worse than epoch 15)
- Val landmark degraded from 0.53px to 0.57px
- Train/val gap widened to 5.7° — clear overfitting

**Assessment**: Phase 3 contributed no validation improvement. The model's best generalization was at epoch 15.

## Key Findings

### 1. Gaze Validation Plateau at ~18°

The model achieved 17.67° val angular error at epoch 15, far from the GazeGene paper's baseline of **3.76° (ResNet-18)** and **5.32° (ResNet-50)**. The 14° gap suggests systematic issues, not just insufficient training.

**Likely causes:**

| Factor | Evidence | Impact |
|--------|----------|--------|
| **No data augmentation** | GazeGene paper uses random translation + color jitter; we use none | High — primary cause of overfitting |
| **Multi-task interference** | Landmark and gaze share backbone; training landmark degrades gaze capacity | Medium — paper trains gaze-only |
| **Multi-view batch structure** | Phase 2+ uses mv_groups x 9 batches (576 samples), reducing sample diversity vs Phase 1 (2048 random samples) | Medium — less diverse batches may accelerate overfitting |
| **Inactive multi-view losses** | gaze_mv stayed flat at ~0.20, contributing gradients that may not align with gaze improvement | Low-Medium — may be adding noise |

### 2. Multi-View Consistency Losses Did Not Converge

The `gaze_mv` (gaze ray consistency) loss remained constant at ~0.20 across all epochs. This means predicted gaze vectors from different camera views of the same subject did NOT converge in world frame. Possible explanations:

- **R_norm inconsistency**: If the normalization rotation matrices don't perfectly map gaze to a common world frame, the loss target is noisy
- **Gaze head capacity**: The OpticalAxisHead may lack the capacity to produce view-independent predictions while sharing a backbone with landmarks
- **Loss weight too low**: At 0.1 weight, gaze_mv may be overwhelmed by the landmark and direct gaze losses
- **Conflicting objectives**: Minimizing per-view gaze L1 (direct supervision) may conflict with cross-view consistency

### 3. Shape Consistency Behaved Differently Per Phase

Shape loss dropped from 0.074 (Phase 2 end) to 0.036 (Phase 3 start) when sigma changed from 1.5 to 1.0. The tighter heatmap sigma made landmarks more precise, naturally improving shape consistency. This is the one multi-view loss that showed responsiveness to training.

## Comparison with GazeGene Baselines

**Important**: The paper has TWO different experiment setups. Our pipeline predicts **optical axis from eye crops**, which is closest to Sec 4.2 (Table 4), NOT Sec 4.1 (Table 2).

### Paper Sec 4.1 — Head Gaze on Full Face (WITH normalization)

| Method | Input | Target | Normalization | Augment | GazeGene Val |
|--------|-------|--------|---------------|---------|--------------|
| ResNet-18 | Full face 224×224 | `gaze_C` (head gaze) | Yes (Zhang [51]) | Translation + jitter | **3.76°** |
| ResNet-50 | Full face 224×224 | `gaze_C` (head gaze) | Yes (Zhang [51]) | Translation + jitter | **5.32°** |
| FullFace [50] | Full face 224×224 | `gaze_C` (head gaze) | Yes (Zhang [51]) | Translation + jitter | **3.54°** |

### Paper Sec 4.2 — Optical Axis on Full Face (WITHOUT normalization)

| Method | Input | Target | Normalization | GazeGene Val |
|--------|-------|--------|---------------|--------------|
| ResNet-18 | Full face 224×224 | `optic_axis_L` + `optic_axis_R` | **No** | **4.87° (L), 4.75° (R)** |
| ResNet-50 | Full face 224×224 | `optic_axis_L` + `optic_axis_R` | **No** | **4.19° (L), 4.05° (R)** |

### Our Pipeline

| Method | Input | Target | Normalization | Augment | GazeGene Val |
|--------|-------|--------|---------------|---------|--------------|
| **RayNet v2** | Eye crop 224×224 | `optic_axis_L` (single eye) | Yes (Zhang) | **None** | **17.67°** |

### Key Differences

1. **Target mismatch**: Sec 4.1 (3.76°) predicts head gaze, NOT optical axis — not directly comparable
2. **Normalization warning**: Paper Sec 4.2 explicitly avoids normalization for optical axis, stating it "introduces distortion along the z-axis"
3. **Input**: Paper uses full face; we use normalized single-eye crops
4. **No augmentation**: Paper applies random translation + color jitter; we had none
5. **Multi-task**: Paper trains gaze-only; we train landmarks + gaze + multiview
6. **GT source**: Previous runs computed optical axis from geometry; paper provides `optic_axis_L` directly in gaze_label

## Recommendations for Next Experiment

### Priority 1: Ablation — Disable Multi-View, Gaze-Only

Run a controlled experiment matching the GazeGene paper methodology:
- Disable multi-view losses entirely (`lam_gaze_consist=0, lam_shape=0`)
- Disable multiview dataloader (use standard random batches throughout)
- Either disable landmarks (`lam_lm=0`) or keep at low weight
- Add data augmentation (color jitter, random translation)
- Use single learning rate 1e-4 for 10 epochs
- Compare with paper's 3.76° (ResNet-18) baseline

This will isolate whether the 18° plateau is caused by multi-view interference or other factors.

**Ablation commands (now available via CLI flags):**

```bash

    --profile a100 --no_multiview --epochs 15

python -m RayNet.train \
```

## Technical Issues Encountered and Resolved

| Issue | Epoch | Root Cause | Fix |
|-------|-------|-----------|-----|
| `svd_cuda_gesvdjBatched not implemented for 'Half'` | 6 (first attempt) | CUDA SVD doesn't support fp16 | Cast to float32 before SVD |
| `reproj=inf, mask=nan` | 6 (second attempt) | Pixel-space geometric operations numerically unstable in fp16 | Rewrote multiview loss to use unit vectors in normalized space |
| NaN loss at batch ~500 | 6 (third attempt) | `torch.acos` gradient singularity at cos_sim=±1 | Replaced with L1 gaze loss + atan2 for metrics |
| Low GPU utilization in Phase 2 | 6-7 | mv_groups=16 gave batch=144 on 80GB GPU | Increased to mv_groups=64 (batch=576) |
